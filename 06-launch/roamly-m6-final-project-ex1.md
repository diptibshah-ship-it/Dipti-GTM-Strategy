- **Model & packaging (The call):** _(I would launch Roamly Groups as a built-in capability of the existing Roamly experience rather than a separate paid subscription.

The goal of the initial launch should be adoption and validation. Core group functionality — shared booking, split payments, and itinerary coordination — should therefore be included when customers book eligible group experiences.

Roamly can monetize indirectly through higher conversion, larger booking values, more travelers per transaction, and increased repeat bookings. Once adoption is proven, Roamly could test premium group services or specialized packages for segments such as corporate groups.)_
- **Research to validate (The proof plan):** _(Before and during launch, I would validate five key assumptions:

Demand: Quantify how many existing sessions show group-booking behavior and which types of travelers exhibit it most often.
Problem: Interview customers who attempted group bookings but abandoned them to understand where coordination breaks down.
Segment: Test whether friend groups and group organizers experience the problem frequently and intensely enough to drive adoption.
Value proposition: Test messaging such as “less coordinating”, “split payments”, and “plan together” to identify which benefit drives the strongest intent.
Product/GTM performance: Track group-booking conversion, abandonment, average booking value, group size, feature adoption, repeat booking, and referrals/invites.

The biggest proof point would be whether reducing coordination friction converts existing group intent into completed bookings.)_
- **Friction points, what was hardest about building this GTM strategy, and how did you work through it?:** _(The hardest part was deciding how narrow to make the initial target audience. Roamly Groups could serve many segments, including families, corporate teams, celebration groups, and travel communities, so it was tempting to position the product broadly.

I worked through this by returning to the strongest evidence in the scenario: existing customers were already trying to coordinate group bookings across separate accounts. That led me to prioritize the segment closest to Roamly's current users rather than selecting a potentially larger but less validated market.

Another challenge was separating features from customer value. “Split payments” is a feature; “you don't have to chase five friends for money” is the customer benefit. Making that distinction helped sharpen the positioning and messaging.)_
- **Your biggest “aha” moment during the project.:** _(My biggest aha was realizing that a GTM strategy does not always need to create new demand. Sometimes the strongest opportunity is already visible in customer behavior.

Roamly customers were effectively trying to create Roamly Groups themselves through workarounds. That changed how I viewed the opportunity: instead of asking, “Who could potentially use this?” I started with, “Who is already trying to solve this problem, and what is stopping them?”_
- **Key takeaways, what changed in how you think about going to market?:** _(The biggest change in my thinking is that GTM is not simply the launch plan that comes after a product is built. It starts much earlier with understanding customer behavior, choosing a specific market opportunity, and making deliberate decisions about who the product is for and why they should care.

I also learned the importance of maintaining a clear thread from customer insight → target segment → positioning → messaging → activation → measurement. When those decisions reinforce each other, the strategy becomes much stronger.

Most importantly, I learned that focus is a strategic choice. A product may eventually serve many audiences, but an effective GTM strategy needs a clear first customer, a specific problem, and a value proposition that speaks directly to that problem.

Your final project now has one consistent thesis: Roamly isn't trying to convince people to travel in groups; people are already doing it. The GTM opportunity is to make Roamly the easiest place for those groups to turn plans into booked experiences.)_
